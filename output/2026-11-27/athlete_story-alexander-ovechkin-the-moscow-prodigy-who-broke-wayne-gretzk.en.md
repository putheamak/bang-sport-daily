# Alexander Ovechkin: The Moscow Prodigy Who Broke Wayne Gretzky's NHL Record

*From a tragic childhood loss in Moscow to 895 NHL goals, the 20-year journey of the Washington Capitals captain.*

**Athlete story** · 2026-11-27

On 6 April 2025, at UBS Arena in Elmont, New York, Alexander Ovechkin took a second-period power-play pass, beat New York Islanders goaltender Ilya Sorokin, and scored the 895th regular-season goal of his NHL career. The strike broke Wayne Gretzky’s league record of 894 goals, an NHL benchmark untouched since 1999, and prompted Gretzky to step onto the ice to congratulate the 39-year-old Washington Capitals captain. Sorokin was the 183rd different goaltender beaten by Ovechkin across 20 NHL seasons in Washington. That record-setting shot completed an arc that began four decades earlier on the outskirts of Moscow, shaped by elite sporting genes, family grief, and two decades in the American capital.

## From Moscow outskirts to Dynamo's records

Ovechkin was born on 17 September 1985 in Moscow into an accomplished athletic family. His mother, Tatyana Ovechkina, won Olympic gold medals with the Soviet basketball team at Montreal 1976 and Moscow 1980, alongside the 1975 FIBA World Championship, while his father, Mikhail Ovechkin, played professional soccer for Dynamo Moscow. At age two, Ovechkin picked up a toy hockey stick, and his mother recounted that whenever hockey broadcasts appeared on television, he refused to let his parents change the channel. The family lived in a high-rise apartment on Moscow's outskirts, where Ovechkin completed eight and a half grades at public school No. 596 under a strict headmaster before entering the Dynamo Moscow youth academy.

When his parents were working, his older brother Sergei drove and escorted him to daily training sessions. In 1995, when Ovechkin was 10 years old, Sergei died from a blood clot following a car accident. His parents insisted Ovechkin play in a youth tournament match scheduled for the following morning, initiating a lifelong ritual where Ovechkin kisses his glove and points skyward after scoring to honor Sergei. At age 11, Ovechkin scored 56 goals in one season in Dynamo's youth system, breaking the previous club academy record of 53 goals established by Pavel Bure. In his Moscow bedroom, Ovechkin collected hockey cards of NHL players, focusing on Pittsburgh Penguins center Mario Lemieux.

## The leap to Washington and "The Goal"

Ovechkin made his senior debut for Dynamo Moscow in the Russian Superleague during the 2001–02 season at age 16, posting four points in 21 games. Across four seasons with Dynamo, he compiled 36 goals and 32 assists in 152 games and helped Dynamo secure the 2004–05 Russian Superleague championship. Florida Panthers general manager Rick Dudley attempted to draft Ovechkin in the ninth round of the 2003 NHL Entry Draft, arguing that leap years made him 18 before the 15 September cutoff, but the league rejected the selection. One year later, on 26 June 2004, the Washington Capitals drafted Ovechkin with the first overall pick.

After the 2004–05 NHL lockout canceled that season, Ovechkin signed a three-year entry-level contract with Washington on 5 August 2005 featuring a base salary of $984,200 with performance bonuses reaching $3.9 million. He debuted in the NHL on 5 October 2005, scoring two goals against Columbus Blue Jackets goaltender Pascal Leclaire in a 3–2 Washington win. On 13 January 2006, Ovechkin recorded his first NHL hat trick against Jean-Sébastien Giguère of the Mighty Ducks of Anaheim. Three days later, on 16 January 2006 against the Phoenix Coyotes, defenseman Paul Mara knocked Ovechkin onto the ice; sliding on his back, Ovechkin rolled, hooked the puck with one hand, and slid it past goalie Brian Boucher for an effort dubbed "The Goal" by commentator Bill Clement. Ovechkin concluded his 2005–06 rookie campaign with 52 goals, 54 assists, 106 points, and a league-leading 425 shots, earning the Calder Memorial Trophy and a First All-Star Team selection.

## Stanley Cup glory and the 895 milestone

Washington appointed Ovechkin the 14th captain in franchise history on 5 January 2010. Over his career, Ovechkin won the Hart Memorial Trophy as league MVP three times (2008, 2009, 2013), three Ted Lindsay Awards (2008, 2009, 2010), and a record nine Maurice "Rocket" Richard Trophies as the league's top regular-season goal scorer. On the international stage, Ovechkin won IIHF World Championship gold medals with Russia in 2008, 2012, and 2014, and skated in three Winter Olympic tournaments in 2006, 2010, and 2014.

His postseason drought ended on 7 June 2018 at T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas, when the Capitals defeated the Vegas Golden Knights 4–3 in Game 5 to win the franchise's first Stanley Cup. Ovechkin led all playoff scorers with 15 goals in 24 games to win the Conn Smythe Trophy as playoff MVP. Off the ice, Ovechkin married Russian model Anastasia Shubskaya in July 2016; the couple have two sons, Sergei, born on 18 August 2018 and named after his late brother, and Ilya, born on 27 May 2020.

During the 2024–25 season, Ovechkin scored 42 goals in 65 games despite missing 16 contests with a fractured left fibula, registering his 14th career 40-goal campaign. When he scored goal number 895 past Sorokin on 6 April 2025, he consolidated NHL all-time records for power-play goals, road goals, overtime goals, and game-winning goals. On 22 March 2026, Ovechkin scored his 1,000th combined goal across regular-season and playoff games, joining Wayne Gretzky as the only two players in NHL history to reach four figures.
