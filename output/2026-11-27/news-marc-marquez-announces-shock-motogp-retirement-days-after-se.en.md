# Marc Marquez Announces Shock MotoGP Retirement Days After Securing Record-Tying Eighth Title

*The 33-year-old Spaniard will race his final Grand Prix in Valencia this weekend, walking away tied with Giacomo Agostini on eight premier-class crowns.*

**News** · 2026-11-27

Eight-time MotoGP world champion Marc Marquez announced his retirement from motorcycle racing on Friday, 27 November 2026, at Circuit Ricardo Tormo in Valencia, Spain. The decision means this weekend's Valencia Grand Prix will serve as the final competitive appearance of his career.

## The garage revelation at Valencia

Marquez delivered the news minutes before the opening practice session of the season finale. Sitting in the Ducati Lenovo Team garage, the 33-year-old held handwritten cards up to television broadcast cameras.

"We'll talk later but first thank you to everyone who has been part of this journey," his message read. "I hope you've enjoyed it as much as I have. This will be my last race in MotoGP. We made history".

Ducati confirmed the departure in an official statement: "Marc Marquez has decided to retire from MotoGP, and the 2026 Valencia Grand Prix will be his final race as a professional rider and with the Ducati Lenovo Team".

## Overcoming a 102-point deficit

The retirement comes five days after Marquez wrapped up the 2026 championship at the Portuguese Grand Prix in Portimão on 22 November, where an eighth-place finish secured the crown. The victory drew Marquez level with Giacomo Agostini's record of eight premier-class championships, breaking his tie with longtime rival Valentino Rossi, who won seven. Across all categories, including his 2010 125cc and 2012 Moto2 championships, it marked his 10th world title.

Marquez secured the 2026 title after falling 102 points behind Aprilia's Marco Bezzecchi early in the campaign. The Spaniard produced the largest points comeback in MotoGP history, winning seven Grands Prix and 10 Sprint races over the remainder of the schedule to take the championship with a round to spare.

## The toll of the comeback

The decision arrives despite Marquez having agreed to a contract extension through the 2028 season ahead of MotoGP's upcoming shift to 850cc engines. Marquez's final seasons followed an extensive rehabilitation process stemming from a fractured right humerus suffered at Jerez in 2020, an injury that required four surgical procedures. He also sustained a shoulder injury at the 2025 Indonesian Grand Prix.

"I arrived in MotoGP in my way, and I will go in my way," Marquez told reporters at a Valencia press conference on Friday afternoon. "The situation this year was very in the limit".
