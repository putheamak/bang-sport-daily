# Svay Rieng Stun Machida as Crown Fall in Kuching: ACL Two Matchday 5 Round-Up

*Svay Rieng beat Japan's Machida Zelvia 2-0 in Phnom Penh, Phnom Penh Crown lost 3-0 in Malaysia, and both Cambodian clubs still lead their own fate into the final round*

**News** · 2026-11-27

Cambodian football had its best and toughest nights in Asia within a few hours of each other on Thursday, 26 November. In Phnom Penh, Preah Khan Reach Svay Rieng beat Japan's FC Machida Zelvia 2-0, handing the Group G leaders their first defeat of the AFC Champions League Two. In Malaysia, Phnom Penh Crown lost 3-0 to Kuching City, but stayed top of Group H.

After five of six group matches, both Cambodian clubs know a draw in the final round on 3 December will take them into the Round of 16. Here is how Matchday 5 went in all eight groups, and what each team said about it.

## Svay Rieng 2-0 Machida Zelvia: Cambodia's night of the season

Svay Rieng played the match at Morodok Techo National Stadium. Machida arrived having won all four group games, including a 2-0 win over Svay Rieng in Japan on the first matchday.

The first half was even. Kwame Peprah shot just wide early on, and goalkeeper Vireak Dara made an important save before half-time, the AFC reported. Tiago Alves had the best chance of the half when he was sent through on goal, but he pulled his shot wide.

Machida changed two players at half-time, but Svay Rieng scored first. In the 59th minute, Alessandro Martins Júnior curled a low shot past goalkeeper Tani from the edge of the box. Thirteen minutes later, Rui Pires lifted the ball over Machida's defence, Alves beat the offside trap and chipped the ball over Tani for 2-0.

Machida pushed for a way back. Futa Tokumura's long shot hit the crossbar and Kim Min-tae's header hit the post, according to the AFC. Dara and his defence kept a clean sheet to the end.

**What worked for Svay Rieng:** they kept their shape against a side that had more of the ball (ESPN's statistics gave Machida 56% possession) and took their chances in a 13-minute spell after the break. Kampuchea Thmey called it the first win by a Cambodian club over a Japanese club, a week after Svay Rieng beat Thailand's Port FC 4-1 in the ASEAN Club Championship.

**What went wrong for Machida:** it was their first defeat in the tournament, and, according to Soccer Digest, their first in 15 competitive matches. Machida coach Go Kuroda pointed to a busy match schedule, the long journey and the heat in Phnom Penh, Kampuchea Thmey reported in Khmer. "When we arrived here, we had to face even hotter weather," he said (translated from Khmer).

Svay Rieng now have 10 points, two behind Machida, who are already through. Their last game is away to Shanghai Shenhua on 3 December. Shenhua have 7 points and must win; a draw is enough for Svay Rieng, Troryorng reported. The first meeting between the two sides in Cambodia ended 0-0.

## Kuching City 3-0 Phnom Penh Crown: punished on the break

In Kuching, the home side started fast. Ajdin Mujagić headed against the post in the opening minutes and had a goal ruled out for offside, Utusan Sarawak reported. Safawi Rasid gave Kuching the lead in the 32nd minute. Before half-time, Kuching goalkeeper Haziq Nadzli made a strong save to keep Crown from equalising.

Crown improved after the break and pushed forward, but were caught on the counter. Jerome Mpacko Etame scored within a minute of coming on for Safawi in the 75th minute, and Dylan Wenzel-Halls added a third in stoppage time.

**What went wrong for Crown:** coach Oleg Starynskyi felt the score was harsher than the game. "It was a tough result because we didn't deserve to lose 3-0," he told the AFC. "They punished us with a counterattack, and that second goal made things difficult," he said, as quoted by Utusan Sarawak (translated from Malay). He added: "The group is very tough."

**What worked for Kuching:** coach Aidil Sharin Sahak praised his players' discipline and effort, and said the result made up for their 2-1 defeat in Phnom Penh earlier in the group. "We didn't deserve to lose in Phnom Penh," he told the AFC, adding that "the team is really growing". Safawi, named man of the match, said the win "isn't just for me, but for the entire team" (translated from Malay, Utusan Sarawak).

On the same night, Gangwon FC beat Kitchee 3-2 in Gangneung. Kitchee led twice, through Everton Camargo in the first minute and Asier Illarramendi early in the second half. Kim Dae-won scored twice for Gangwon, and Shin Min-ha headed the winner from Kim's cross in stoppage time, the AFC reported.

Group H now reads: Phnom Penh Crown 9 points, Kuching City 7, Gangwon 7, Kitchee 5. On 3 December, Crown host Gangwon and Kuching visit Kitchee. On these numbers, a draw would be enough for Crown to go through. A defeat would put them out only if Kuching also beat Kitchee.

## Around the East

**Group E:** Persib Bandung beat FC Seoul 3-1 in Bandung to make it five wins out of five and finish top. Seoul, the Korean champions, rotated their squad, levelled through Leonardo Ruiz but conceded again before half-time and lost Moon Seon-min to a red card in the 70th minute, the AFC reported. "Good defence, good transitions, three goals," said Persib coach Igor Tolic (translated from Indonesian, Antara). Seoul coach Kim Gi-dong said he wanted to apologise to the supporters (translated, JPNN). In Geelong, Melbourne Victory and The Cong-Viettel drew 0-0. Victory missed several good chances, and coach Giovanni Savarese said his team needed to be more "killers" in front of goal. The draw put Viettel out. Persib have 15 points, Seoul 6, Victory 5 and Viettel 2. Seoul host Victory on 2 December.

**Group F:** Singapore's Lion City Sailors won 3-0 away to BG Pathum United, with goals from Ilhan Fandi, Hugo Gomes and Matthias Phaëton, to win the group with a game to spare. "It was a collective performance throughout the game," said coach Mark Jackson. Pathum coach Peerapong Pichitchotirat admitted: "We have to accept that we weren't the better team today." Adelaide United won 7-1 away to Tai Po in Hong Kong, with four goals from 20-year-old Burkitt; Tai Po had Paulinho sent off. Adelaide coach Airton Andrioli kept calm: "We've achieved nothing at the moment." The Sailors have 13 points, Adelaide 9 and Pathum 7. Adelaide host Pathum in the final round.

**Group G:** Shanghai Shenhua beat Tampines Rovers 7-1 in Singapore. Xie Pengfei scored a hat-trick, Rafael Ratão and Luis Asué two each. Tampines scored through Seiga Sumi but had Amirul Haikal sent off early in the second half for a foul in the box; Shenhua's Wilson Manafa hit the crossbar with the penalty. The result keeps Shenhua's hopes alive for their final-round meeting with Svay Rieng.

## Around the West

**Group A:** Al Wahda came from behind to win 2-1 at Kuwait SC and are through as group winners on 12 points. Kuwait led through Ibrahim Kamil, but Wahda scored twice in the last half hour. "We will fight until the very end," said coach Péricles Chamusca. Nasaf beat Al Khaldiya 1-0 in Qarshi through an Abbosjon Gulomov goal early in the second half, which put Khaldiya out. Nasaf coach Ruziqul Berdiyev complained that "VAR is not working in the tournament" after a late incident (zamin.uz). Nasaf (7) visit Wahda on 9 December; Kuwait (6) need to win and hope Nasaf do not.

**Group B:** Al Jazira beat Gol Gohar 1-0 with a 40th-minute shot from Anderson Talisca and are through on 13 points. Turkmenistan's Arkadag joined them by winning 4-2 at Al Muharraq, who had Bruno Santos sent off in the third minute and played almost the whole match with ten men. Dayanc Meredow scored twice. Arkadag (10) host Jazira for first place.

**Group C:** Aleksandar Mitrović volleyed in after the Al Faisaly goalkeeper spilled a shot, giving Al Rayyan a 1-0 win in Amman. Al Taawoun, who rested several key players, were held 0-0 at home by Al Nahda; Abderrazak Hamdallah came off the bench and hit the woodwork. Rayyan (12) and Taawoun (11) are both through and meet to decide first place.

**Group D:** East Bengal beat Jordan's Al Hussein 1-0 in Kolkata with an 87th-minute goal from a corner by Jeakson Singh, the first ACL Two win by an Indian club, according to Khel Now and The Open Press. Al Hussein dominated, hit the woodwork four times, and were denied by goalkeeper Phurba Lachenpa. "For the first time in this competition, we had luck," admitted coach Antonio Habas. Oman's Al Seeb beat Al Shorta 2-0, the Iraqi side's first defeat of the group stage, although Shorta are already through on 10 points. Seeb (7) visit Al Hussein (5) on 8 December, with East Bengal (5) away to Shorta.

## What comes next

The East groups finish on 2 and 3 December, the West groups on 8 and 9 December. The top two in each group reach the Round of 16. For Cambodia, it comes down to two matches on the same day: Svay Rieng in China and Crown at home to Gangwon, each needing a draw.
