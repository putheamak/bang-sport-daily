# Yelena Isinbayeva: The Gymnast Who Redefined Women's Pole Vaulting

*From a Volgograd gym to 30 world records and two Olympic titles, the Russian star transformed her event before a painful finish in 2016.*

**Athlete story** · 2026-11-27

Yelena Isinbayeva arrived in senior track and field at a moment when women's pole vaulting was still defining its boundaries, and over the course of 18 years she pushed those limits higher than any woman before her. Born on 3 June 1982 in Volgograd to a Tabasaran father and a Russian mother, she spent a decade preparing for an entirely different sport. Between the ages of five and 15, Isinbayeva trained as an artistic gymnast. By 1997, her height had reached 1.74 metres, a frame gymnastics coaches deemed uncompetitive for the apparatus. That obstacle redirected her athletic trajectory. Her gymnastics coach, Alexander Lisovoy, contacted track and field coach Yevgeny Trofimov, who introduced the 15-year-old to the pole vault—an event so new to her that she initially did not recognize the name of men's world record holder Sergey Bubka.

The transition was rapid. Gymnastics provided Isinbayeva with upper-body strength, spatial awareness and core discipline, allowing Trofimov to focus immediately on runway speed and the mechanics of the plant. Six months after picking up a pole, Isinbayeva won gold at the 1998 World Youth Games in Moscow with a jump of 4.00 metres. A year later, she won the 1999 World Youth Championships in Bydgoszcz at 4.10 metres. In 2000, she claimed the World Junior Championships title in Santiago, clearing 4.20 metres, before making an early senior exit in qualifying at the Sydney Olympics.

## The five-metre barrier and Athens gold

Isinbayeva’s senior breakthrough intensified in 2003 through a domestic rivalry with compatriot Svetlana Feofanova. On 13 July 2003 in Gateshead, England, she set her first senior outdoor world record with a clearance of 4.82 metres, taking world bronze later that summer in Paris. In 2004, the duel pushed both athletes upward. After Feofanova broke the mark in Heraklion, Isinbayeva responded with 4.89 metres in Birmingham and 4.90 metres at Crystal Palace in London. At the 2004 Athens Olympics, Isinbayeva secured gold with a world record of 4.91 metres, later extending it to 4.92 metres in Brussels to conclude an eight-record season that earned her IAAF World Athlete of the Year honours.

Her defining career milestone arrived on 22 July 2005 at London’s Crystal Palace. Having cleared 4.96 metres, she requested the bar be raised to 5.00 metres—a height no female vaulter had ever attempted in competition. She cleared it on her first attempt. Three weeks later at the 2005 World Championships in Helsinki, she won gold by clearing 5.01 metres in cold rain, establishing a 41-centimetre victory margin over the rest of the field. Over her career, she won World Championship titles across youth, junior and senior levels, a distinction shared with only ten other track and field athletes in history.

## Beijing dominance, the 2010 hiatus and Moscow redemption

Isinbayeva continued her dominance into the 2007 season, winning 18 out of 18 meets, defending her world title in Osaka and claiming the IAAF Golden League jackpot. At the 2008 Beijing Olympics, in front of 91,000 spectators at the National Stadium, she retained her Olympic title with a third-attempt world record of 5.05 metres, finishing 25 centimetres ahead of American silver medalist Jenn Suhr.

A sharp reversal followed in 2009. At the World Championships in Berlin, Isinbayeva failed at 4.75 metres once and 4.80 metres twice, recording no height and leaving the stadium without a medal. Nine days later in Zürich, on 28 August 2009, she rebounded by clearing 5.06 metres, setting an outdoor world record that remains unbeaten. Yet after finishing fourth at the 2010 World Indoor Championships in Doha, exhaustion prompted her to take an open-ended hiatus from competition.

Isinbayeva reunited with original coach Trofimov in Volgograd in 2011. At the 2012 London Olympics, she took bronze with a clearance of 4.70 metres. Her intended competitive farewell came before a home crowd at the 2013 World Championships in Moscow. At Luzhniki Stadium, she cleared 4.89 metres on her first attempt to defeat Suhr and Cuba’s Yarisley Silva, claiming her third outdoor world title before running a victory lap wrapped in the Russian flag.

## Rio exclusion, family life and Tenerife

After Moscow, Isinbayeva stepped away to start a family, marrying Russian javelin thrower Nikita Petinov and giving birth to their daughter, Eva, in June 2014. She returned to training in 2015 targeting the 2016 Rio de Janeiro Olympics. However, the IAAF suspended the Russian athletics federation following the McLaren Report into state-sponsored doping. While Isinbayeva was never implicated in personal doping infractions, the Court of Arbitration for Sport upheld the blanket team ban, barring her from competing in Rio.

On 19 August 2016 in Rio de Janeiro, Isinbayeva announced her retirement from active sport at age 34, having been elected by her peers to an eight-year term on the IOC Athletes' Commission. In 2018, she gave birth to a son, Dobrynya. In 2021, she relocated with her family to Tenerife in Spain’s Canary Islands. Today, she oversees a charitable foundation funding youth athletic facilities in Volgograd and maintains a private family life, while her 5.06-metre outdoor mark stands into its second decade as the ceiling of women's pole vaulting.
