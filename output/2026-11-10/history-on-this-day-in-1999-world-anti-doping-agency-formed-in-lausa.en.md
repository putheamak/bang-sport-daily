# On This Day in 1999: World Anti-Doping Agency Formed in Lausanne

*Born from the Declaration of Lausanne, the international watchdog was established to coordinate the global fight against performance-enhancing drugs in elite sport.*

**On this day** · 2026-11-10

On November 10, 1999, sports administrators and government representatives gathered in the Olympic capital of Lausanne, Switzerland, to establish a unified international framework for clean competition. The meeting led to the formal creation of the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA), an organization born from a collective initiative involving the International Olympic Committee (IOC) and national governments from over 140 nations.

The establishment of the agency followed growing international concern over performance-enhancing substances in elite athletics and built directly upon the momentum generated by the "Declaration of Lausanne." Initially headquartered in Switzerland, WADA later moved its primary administrative base to Montreal, Quebec, Canada, in 2002, while maintaining its Lausanne office as the regional hub for Europe. Additional regional offices were subsequently established in Africa, Asia/Oceania, and Latin America.

## The World Anti-Doping Code

Central to WADA's institutional mandate is the World Anti-Doping Code, a standardized regulatory document designed to harmonize anti-doping policies across international sports federations and national organizations. The code sets out comprehensive guidelines covering prohibited substances, testing protocols, investigations, laboratories, Therapeutic Use Exemptions, and results management.

The document has achieved widespread adoption across the international sporting landscape. More than 650 sports organizations—including the IOC, the International Paralympic Committee, and numerous international federations and national anti-doping bodies—are signatories to the code. Technical analysis for doping control is carried out by approximately 30 WADA-accredited laboratories worldwide, while the Court of Arbitration for Sport holds ultimate judicial jurisdiction over doping-related cases.

Governance of the agency reflects a dual structure divided equally between representatives of the sports movement, including current and former athletes, and public authorities from worldwide governments. The highest decision-making authority is the 38-member Foundation Board, which appoints the agency's president, while day-to-day operations are managed by a 16-member executive committee.

## Evolution and Investigations

Over the decades, the regulatory framework has undergone several major revisions. In November 2007, over 600 sports organizations unanimously adopted a revised code at the Third World Conference on Doping in Sport, which took effect in January 2009. Further amendments approved in 2013 doubled the standard sanction for intentional first-time doping offenses while introducing provisions for more lenient penalties in cases of inadvertent violations or active cooperation.

The agency's enforcement and investigative capacities faced a major test in 2016, when independent investigator Richard McLaren published a report detailing state-sponsored doping involving more than 1,000 Russian athletes across more than 30 sports between 2011 and 2015. The findings led to significant international sanctions and barred numerous competitors from subsequent Olympic events, though later decisions regarding the reinstatement of athletes drew sharp public debate from whistleblowers and legal representatives alike.

Today, WADA continues to oversee global anti-doping capacities through scientific research, education, and compliance monitoring, maintaining its role as a central pillar in the governance of international sport.
