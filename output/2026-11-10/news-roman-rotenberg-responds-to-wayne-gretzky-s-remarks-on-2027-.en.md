# Roman Rotenberg Responds to Wayne Gretzky's Remarks on 2027 World Junior Championship

*Russian Ice Hockey Federation official comments on Canadian policy and hockey legend's public stance.*

**News** · 2026-11-10

## Public Remarks on International Restrictions

On November 10, 2026, Roman Rotenberg, the First Vice-President of the Russian Ice Hockey Federation, publicly addressed recent statements made by hockey legend Wayne Gretzky regarding the 2027 World Junior Ice Hockey Championship. Gretzky had publicly supported the stance of Canadian leadership regarding the exclusion of Russian and Belarusian athletes from entering Canada for international tournaments.

Canadian authorities previously indicated that entry visas would not be issued to athletes representing Russia and Belarus for competitions hosted within the country. When asked about these restrictions, Gretzky publicly endorsed the approach, stating that many Canadian citizens and players shared the perspective.

## Rotenberg's Assessment of the Situation

Reacting to the remarks, Rotenberg pointed to Gretzky's heritage, noting his Belarusian grandfather and upbringing within a family familiar with the sport's traditions. However, Rotenberg attributed the public stance to diplomatic necessity rather than personal conviction, suggesting external pressures shape such commentary.

"He is observing politeness, is friends with the prime minister of Canada, and is friends with Donald Trump. He needs to maintain a neutral position," Rotenberg stated, as reported by Sports.ru. He added that the hockey legend operates within a specific social and political environment in North America, suggesting that public figures often align their statements with local expectations to maintain their standing within that community.

## Wider Context of International Competitions

The discussion surrounds preparations for the 2027 World Junior Championship and the ongoing restrictions impacting youth and senior international ice hockey tournaments. International sports federations and host governments continue to navigate complex geopolitical landscapes regarding the participation of athletes from specific nations.

While public debates persist among former players, administrators, and political leaders, sports organizations face ongoing challenges in balancing international competition rules with national immigration policies. The latest remarks highlight the intersection of sports diplomacy and geopolitical pressures affecting upcoming global tournaments, as federations and host nations address visa requirements and entry permissions for scheduled international events.
