# UFC 334 incident: Bruce Buffer and Israel Adesanya weigh in on Josh Hokit gesture

*The veteran ring announcer addresses a mid-fight broadcast interaction as high-profile figures react to the viral moment*

**News** · 2026-11-16

## What happened
During the proceedings of UFC 334, heavyweight fighter Josh Hokit directed a middle-finger gesture toward veteran UFC ring announcer Bruce Buffer. The incident occurred in the immediate aftermath of the contest, capturing the attention of the live audience and viewers watching the broadcast worldwide. The moment, which was caught on camera, quickly gained traction across social media platforms and sports news outlets, turning a post-fight sequence into a significant talking point.

## Why it is being talked about
Bruce Buffer has served as the voice of the UFC for decades, becoming a central, recognizable figure in the promotion's presentation. Because of his stature and the professional nature of his role, any direct confrontation involving him is rare and draws immediate scrutiny. The incident is being discussed primarily because it involves a breach of the traditional decorum expected between competitors and on-air personnel. The situation reached a wider audience when former UFC middleweight champion Israel Adesanya publicly reacted to the footage. Adesanya’s commentary, alongside the general discourse surrounding athlete conduct, has elevated the incident beyond a standard post-fight interaction.

## Key facts and numbers
UFC 334 served as the host event for this exchange. Bruce Buffer, responding to the gesture and the broader context of the interaction, stated, "I can only take so much." This comment has been widely reported as a direct response to the lack of respect shown toward him during the live broadcast. The incident highlights the high-intensity nature of the sport and the friction that can occur under the pressures of a live event. While Josh Hokit was the primary participant in the gesture, the resulting discussion has drawn in senior voices within the MMA community, including Israel Adesanya, to weigh in on the boundaries of professional conduct.

## What happens next
As of November 16, 2026, the UFC organization has not released an official statement regarding potential disciplinary action or policy changes regarding athlete interactions with broadcast staff. The promotion's standard procedures for fighter conduct remain in effect, and industry analysts are monitoring to see if the organization will implement new guidelines to prevent similar occurrences during future broadcasts. For now, the incident remains a point of contention and discussion among fans and media, with no immediate administrative resolution confirmed by the UFC.
