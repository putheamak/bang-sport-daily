# Sorn Seavmey: The Path of Cambodia's Taekwondo Pioneer

*From a family of martial artists to the top of the Asian Games podium, Sorn Seavmey has defined a new era for Cambodian sport.*

**Athlete story** · 2026-11-13

Born on September 14, 1995, Sorn Seavmey emerged from a family deeply embedded in the martial arts landscape of Cambodia. Alongside her brother, Sorn Elit, and sister, Sorn Davin, Seavmey developed her craft within a household that prioritized the discipline and rigorous physical demands of taekwondo. Standing 183 cm tall, her physical stature provided an initial advantage, but it was the technical consistency honed through years of training with her siblings that allowed her to transition from a domestic practitioner to an international competitor.

## The Incheon Breakthrough

The defining moment of her career occurred during the 2014 Asian Games in Incheon, South Korea. Entering the women’s under-73 kg division, Seavmey faced a high-pressure environment representing a nation that had not secured an Asian Games gold medal since its first participation in 1954. Her journey through the tournament was marked by decisive performance shifts. In the opening round, she dominated her opponent from Uzbekistan with a score of 29–7. 

Advancing to the semi-final, she faced a significantly closer contest against an opponent from the Philippines, narrowly prevailing 6–5. The final match against her Iranian opponent concluded with a 7–4 victory. This result secured the first Asian Games gold medal in Cambodian history, transforming Seavmey into a national icon and establishing a new benchmark for Cambodian participation in continental multi-sport events.

## Olympic Representation and Regional Consistency

Following her success in Incheon, Seavmey continued to navigate the elite international circuit. She qualified for the 2016 Summer Olympics in Rio de Janeiro, where she competed in the women’s +67 kg division. Her role in the Olympic movement extended beyond the mat; she was selected as the flag bearer for Cambodia during the Parade of Nations. While her Olympic run ended in the round of 16 following a defeat by Reshmie Oogink of the Netherlands, her qualification itself represented a major milestone for Cambodian taekwondo.

Her ability to maintain form over multiple years was most evident in the Southeast Asian (SEA) Games. She secured gold medals in 2013 in Myanmar and again in 2017 in Malaysia. These victories reinforced her standing as a dominant force in the regional weight categories. Her leadership role within the Cambodian delegation continued into 2018, when she again served as the flag bearer for the opening ceremony of the Asian Games.

## Continued Success on Home Soil

Nearly a decade after her breakthrough in Incheon, Seavmey demonstrated her enduring competitive longevity during the 2023 SEA Games, which were hosted in Cambodia. Competing in the women’s over 73 kg division, she secured another gold medal by defeating Sirimanotham Sonesavnh of the Lao People’s Democratic Republic with a score of 2–0. 

This victory on home soil underscored a career trajectory that spans from her early development with her siblings to her status as a veteran of the sport. Throughout her career, Seavmey has remained a central figure in the National Olympic Committee of Cambodia’s efforts to develop elite-level martial arts talent. Her legacy is defined not only by the medals collected across three different SEA Games and the Asian Games but also by her consistent presence at the forefront of the nation's international sporting efforts.
