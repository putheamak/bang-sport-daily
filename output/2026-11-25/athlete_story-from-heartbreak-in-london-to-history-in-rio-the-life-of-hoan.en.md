# From Heartbreak in London to History in Rio: The Life of Hoang Xuan Vinh

*Overcoming childhood loss, severe nearsightedness and agonizing near-misses, the army officer delivered his country's first Olympic gold medal.*

**Athlete story** · 2026-11-25

On August 6, 2016, inside the Olympic Shooting Centre in Deodoro, Rio de Janeiro, 41-year-old military officer Hoang Xuan Vinh stood at firing point eight, trailing Brazilian shooter Felipe Almeida Wu by 0.2 points ahead of the twentieth and final shot of the men's 10-metre air pistol final. After Wu registered a 10.1 to ignite the home crowd, Xuan Vinh took several deep breaths, steadied his pistol, and delivered a 10.7 into the inner ring. The shot lifted his total to 202.5 points, established an Olympic record, and secured the first Olympic gold medal in the history of Vietnamese sports.

Four days later, Xuan Vinh added an Olympic silver medal in the 50-metre free pistol behind South Korea's Jin Jong-oh with a score of 191.3. That campaign in Brazil ended a 64-year Olympic medal drought for Vietnam, but the road to that podium was marked by family loss, material scarcity, and repeated competitive heartbreak.

## The Barracks and a Late Calling

Born on October 6, 1974, in Son Tay, Xuan Vinh experienced tragedy early when his mother, a factory worker, died when he was three years old. His father, a soldier in the Vietnam People's Army, moved Xuan Vinh and his toddler sister into a small apartment in Hanoi, raising them on modest military wages. Following high school, Xuan Vinh enlisted in the armed forces, studying at the Military Engineering Officer School in Song Be province and training for nearly two years at Army Officer School 2.

Graduating in 1994, he received an assignment to the 239th Engineer Brigade in Thuong Tin, Ha Tay province. While performing his duties as an officer, his precision with military service rifles caught the attention of superiors during intra-army tournaments. In 1998, Xuan Vinh won first place at the national military shooting competition. The Army Club recruited him the following year, and at age 26 he transferred from active field command to join the national shooting programme. His late entrance into high-performance sport came alongside a physical handicap unusual for elite marksmen: Xuan Vinh competed while being severely nearsighted, using corrective shooting lenses to see the target.

Domestic and regional success followed quickly. In 1999, Xuan Vinh took bronze in the men's air pistol team event at the National Cup in Hai Phong. In 2000, the year he married Phan Huong Giang, he broke the national record in the 10-metre air pistol with 580 points to win national gold. Beginning at the 2001 Southeast Asian Games in Malaysia and running through the 2011 Games in Indonesia, Xuan Vinh captured at least one gold medal across six consecutive SEA Games editions, highlighted by a three-gold haul in Thailand in 2007.

## Agony by One-Tenth of a Point

Despite regional dominance, Xuan Vinh's transition to world-level competition was haunted by late-round collapses. At the 2010 Asian Games in Guangzhou, he held first place in the men's 25-metre standard pistol before misfiring on his final shot during the rapid-fire stage, tumbling from the lead to 13th place.

Two years later, Xuan Vinh qualified for the 2012 London Olympics via the Asian Championship in Qatar. In London, he missed the 10-metre air pistol final by one point, finishing ninth with 582 points. In the 50-metre pistol final, Xuan Vinh entered the ninth shot needing only eight points to lock up an Olympic medal. He recorded a 7.3. Despite a 10.2 on his tenth shot for a 658.5 total, he missed the bronze medal by 0.1 points to China's Wang Zhiwei.

Devastated by the margin, Xuan Vinh contemplated retirement. Instead, national coach Nguyen Thi Nhung instituted an unorthodox regimen to harden his mental discipline. Before daily workouts, Xuan Vinh was required to shout aloud: "I am an Olympic medallist". To master stillness under pressure, he spent one full year practicing a routine of standing upright and motionless for two continuous hours without speaking. Training conditions in Hanoi remained tight, with Xuan Vinh often limited to 100 live rounds per day—a fraction of the ammunition used by rivals in Europe and East Asia. South Korean coach Park Chung-gun joined the coaching staff in late 2014, organizing overseas camps in South Korea so Xuan Vinh could practice on modern electronic scoring targets.

The changes bore fruit. In late 2012, Xuan Vinh won Vietnam's first Asian Air Gun Championship gold in China with 683.3 points. At the 2013 ISSF World Cup in Changwon, South Korea, he avenged his London loss by edging Wang Zhiwei with 200.8 points to claim Vietnam's first World Cup title.

## Legacy Beyond Rio

Following his historic double-medal performance at Rio 2016, Xuan Vinh received promotion to the rank of colonel in the Vietnam People's Army and was voted Vietnam's Outstanding Athlete of 2016. He competed at his third Summer Games at the Tokyo Olympics in 2021, where he placed 22nd in the 10-metre air pistol qualifying round with 573 points.

After retiring from international competition, Colonel Xuan Vinh moved into coaching as the head coach of the Army Sports Shooting Team and an instructor within the national squad. In 2023, he stood behind the line at the 19th Asian Games in Hangzhou, China, guiding his pupil Pham Quang Huy to the men's 10-metre air pistol gold medal—Vietnam's first Asian Games shooting title in the event. Today, Xuan Vinh directs the Army shooting programme from Hanoi, mentoring junior military shooters pursuing qualification for future Olympic Games.
