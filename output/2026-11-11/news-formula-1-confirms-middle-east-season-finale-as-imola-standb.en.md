# Formula 1 confirms Middle East season finale as Imola standby plan ends

*F1 proceeds with Qatar and Abu Dhabi rounds after standing down Italian circuit, while maintaining that safety remains the ultimate priority.*

**News** · 2026-11-11

## What happened
Formula One management has officially informed the Imola circuit in Italy that its services will not be required as a back-up venue for the end of the 2026 season. F1 is pressing ahead with its scheduled double-header in the Middle East, with the Qatar Grand Prix slated for November 27–29 and the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix concluding the campaign on December 4–6. 

The Italian venue, located 20 miles from Bologna, had been asked earlier to prepare for a potential grand prix on the Abu Dhabi dates should travel to the region become untenable due to security concerns.

## Why it is being talked about
The decision to proceed follows months of uncertainty stemming from the regional conflict involving the United States, Israel, and Iran. Earlier in the year, the war disrupted the early calendar, forcing the cancellation of the April race in Saudi Arabia and the rescheduling of the Bahrain Grand Prix, which was eventually held at Malaysia's Sepang circuit in October. 

While government travel advice currently allows travel and insurance remains valid, senior figures in the paddock have voiced unease over proximity to the Strait of Hormuz, where missile strikes continue. Furthermore, Saudi Arabia remains the target of attacks from Houthi rebels in Yemen, leading to cancellations or postponements of several events in the country in recent weeks. 

Despite these anxieties, F1 management and teams noted that the situation in Qatar and the United Arab Emirates has stabilized in recent months. Business has returned to normal levels, and both nations hold significant financial and ownership ties within the sport, including Qatari sovereign investment in the Audi team and Abu Dhabi's stake in McLaren.

## Key facts and numbers
Imola was asked on Tuesday to stand down its preparations as the deadline passed. Key stakeholders, including team representatives, met to discuss contingency plans and seek assurances should an escalation occur during the race weeks. 

FIA President Mohammed Ben Sulayem issued a statement on Instagram affirming full commitment to delivering the final two grands prix. Additionally, MotoGP successfully staged a grand prix in Qatar the previous weekend. F1 teams have consistently expressed confidence in the governing body and management to make appropriate scheduling decisions.

## What happens next
Logistical and freight preparations for Qatar and Abu Dhabi will proceed as planned. Formula One management maintains that safety is the absolute priority, emphasizing that the sport remains ready to cancel or alter the events if the security situation deteriorates before the race weekends.
