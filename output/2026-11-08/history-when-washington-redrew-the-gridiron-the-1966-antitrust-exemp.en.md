# When Washington Redrew the Gridiron: The 1966 Antitrust Exemption

*On November 8, 1966, U.S. President Lyndon B. Johnson signed an antitrust exemption that cleared the path for the NFL and AFL to unite, reshaping professional football forever.*

**On this day** · 2026-11-08

On November 8, 1966, the landscape of American professional sports shifted permanently inside the Oval Office. U.S. President Lyndon B. Johnson signed into law a crucial antitrust exemption, paving the way for the National Football League and the upstart American Football League to merge into a single, unified juggernaut.

The stroke of the president's pen ended years of bitter financial warfare between the two rival leagues. Before the legislative intervention, the fierce competition for talent and stadium rights had driven player salaries to unsustainable heights and threatened to bankrupt multiple franchises on both sides. 

## The Legislative Path to Unity

The road to the November 8 legislation required delicate political maneuvering in Washington, D.C. The established NFL, operating under strict federal antitrust scrutiny, needed legal protection from the U.S. Department of Justice to absorb its aggressive young competitor without violating federal competition law.

By attaching the merger approval to an unrelated tax bill, congressional leaders and league executives secured the necessary votes. President Johnson, known for his formidable legislative prowess as a former Senate Majority Leader, signed the measure into law, effectively granting the combined entity a limited antitrust exemption specifically tailored to allow the absorption of AFL teams.

## A New Era for the Sport

The agreement stipulated that the two leagues would maintain separate regular-season schedules before merging completely under the NFL banner in 1970. It also guaranteed the survival of all existing franchises, laying the groundwork for the modern conference structure that fans recognize today.

The result leaves a legacy that extends far beyond the boardroom battles of the 1960s. The merger directly enabled the creation of the common championship game—now known globally as the Super Bowl—and transformed professional football into the most commercially successful sports league in the United States, influencing sports broadcasting and entertainment economies worldwide.
