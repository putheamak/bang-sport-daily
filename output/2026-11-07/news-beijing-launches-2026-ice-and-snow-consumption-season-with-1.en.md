# Beijing Launches 2026 Ice and Snow Consumption Season with 17 New Tourism Routes

*The initiative coincides with the 13th Beijing Citizens Happy Ice and Snow Season, featuring 20,000 public consumption vouchers and major upcoming winter sports events.*

**News** · 2026-11-07

The 2026 Beijing Ice and Snow Consumption Season officially launched on Friday, November 7, highlighting winter tourism and sports participation across the Chinese capital. Organizers unveiled 17 curated ice and snow tourism routes designed to connect the city's premier winter sports venues, cultural landmarks, and commercial hubs.

Alongside the tourism promotion, the 13th Beijing Citizens Happy Ice and Snow Season kicked off. As part of ongoing efforts to maintain the legacy of the 2022 Winter Olympics and encourage widespread physical activity, local authorities announced the distribution of 20,000 public consumption vouchers to lower the financial barrier for residents engaging in winter recreation.

## Building on Olympic Legacy

The dual launch marks a significant step in transforming Beijing's seasonal weather into a sustainable economic and social driver. Since hosting the Winter Olympic Games, the city has continuously integrated elite competition venues with mass participation programs. 

The timing aligns with a busy calendar of winter sports events in the capital. The International Skating Union (ISU) Speed Skating World Cup is set to arrive in Beijing, featuring a host nation roster spearheaded by prominent Olympians Ning Zhongyan and Gao Tingyu.

## Broadening Winter Access

By combining elite international competitions with grassroots initiatives like the Happy Ice and Snow Season, Beijing aims to deepen public engagement with winter disciplines. The newly released 17 tourism routes link urban skating rinks with suburban ski resorts, encouraging both domestic and international visitors to explore the region's cold-weather infrastructure.

Analysts note that targeted measures such as consumption vouchers play a vital role in sustaining year-round utility for Olympic venues, ensuring that facilities constructed for elite sport continue to serve local communities and youth development programs for years to come.
