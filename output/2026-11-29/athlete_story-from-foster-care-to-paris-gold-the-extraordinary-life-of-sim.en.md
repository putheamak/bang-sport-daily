# From Foster Care to Paris Gold: The Extraordinary Life of Simone Biles

*Adopted at six and tested by adversity, the American overcame trauma and the 'twisties' to collect 41 global medals.*

**Athlete story** · 2026-11-29

A rain-diverted day-care field trip in Houston introduced a six-year-old girl to a gymnastics hall in 2003. When instructors watched her copy the older athletes on the tumbling strip, they sent a letter home urging her family to enroll her in classes. Twenty-one years later, inside Bercy Arena at the 2024 Paris Olympics, Simone Biles stood atop the medal podium with her 11th Olympic medal around her neck, having accumulated 30 World Championship medals to establish herself as the most decorated gymnast in the history of the sport.

The trajectory from a chaotic childhood to athletic supremacy was shaped by resilience long before she ever mounted a balance beam at an Olympic Games. Born on March 14, 1997, in Columbus, Ohio, Biles was the third of four children. Her biological mother, Shanon Biles, struggled with substance abuse and was unable to care for the family, leaving Simone and her siblings to spend their early years in and out of the foster care system.

## A Texas Sanctuary and an Unshakable Foundation

In 2000, her maternal grandfather, Ron Biles, and his wife, Nellie Cayetano Biles, learned that the children were in foster care and brought them to their home in Spring, Texas, a northern suburb of Houston. By 2003, Ron and Nellie formally adopted Simone and her younger sister, Adria, while Ron’s sister Harriet adopted the two oldest siblings. Through Nellie, who grew up in Belize, Biles gained dual citizenship and a tight-knit family culture rooted in Catholic faith and Sunday family dinners.

Once enrolled at Bannon's Gymnastics, Biles met coach Aimee Boorman at age eight. The coach recognized that Biles possessed a rare combination of explosive power and aerial awareness. In 2012, at age 15, Biles made the pivotal choice to leave public high school for homeschooling. The move increased her training schedule from 20 to 32 hours per week and allowed her to earn her high-school diploma in 2015 while pursuing international competition.

Her elite senior breakthrough almost derailed before it began. In July 2013, at the U.S. Classic in Chicago, Biles suffered a disastrous meet: she fell off multiple apparatuses and twisted her ankle on floor exercise, unable to finish the competition. Shaken by self-doubt, she began consulting a sports psychologist. The mental recalibration produced immediate dividends. Weeks later, Biles captured her first U.S. national all-around title. In October 2013, at the World Championships in Antwerp, Belgium, the 16-year-old won the individual all-around gold medal, becoming the first African American woman to achieve the feat.

## Redefining Physics and Reaching the Rio Pinnacle

Between 2013 and the 2016 Rio Olympics, Biles dominated women's artistic gymnastics without dropping an all-around competition. In 2015, she turned professional, forfeiting a scholarship to UCLA. In Rio de Janeiro, leading the U.S. squad dubbed the "Final Five," Biles delivered four gold medals—in team, individual all-around, vault, and floor exercise—alongside a bronze on the balance beam. 

Her technical prowess forced the International Gymnastics Federation to continually update its scoring code. Five distinct elements across vault, balance beam, and floor exercise now bear her name, each requiring unprecedented acrobatic difficulty. Her signature moves include the "Biles II" on floor—a triple-twisting double-tucked somersault—and the "Biles II" on vault, a Yurchenko double pike that no other female gymnast had ever executed in international competition.

Yet the mounting physical and emotional toll culminated at the Tokyo Olympics in 2021. Favored to win multiple golds, Biles suffered a disorienting mental block known as the "twisties" during the vault rotation of the team final. Lacking air awareness and risking catastrophic physical injury, she withdrew from the team final and four individual event finals. While critics questioned her decision, Biles prioritized her health, returning on the final day of artistic gymnastics to secure a bronze medal on the balance beam, alongside the team silver.

## The Courage to Pause and the Triumph in Paris

The Tokyo withdrawal ignited a global conversation regarding athlete welfare and mental health. In September 2021, Biles testified before the U.S. Senate Judiciary Committee about the systemic institutional failures surrounding the abuse scandal involving former team doctor Larry Nassar, directly confronting USA Gymnastics and the FBI over their negligence. In July 2022, President Joe Biden presented her with the Presidential Medal of Freedom, making her the youngest recipient of the honor at age 25.

Away from the gymnasium, Biles rebuilt her personal life. She met NFL safety Jonathan Owens in 2020, and the couple married in Houston on April 22, 2023. After a two-year hiatus from competition, Biles returned to the gym with coaches Laurent and Cécile Landi, implementing weekly therapy sessions as a permanent fixture of her training regimen.

Her competitive return in 2023 yielded a record-breaking eighth national all-around championship—shattering a 90-year-old U.S. record—followed by a ninth title in 2024 at age 27, making her the oldest woman to win the championship. At the 2024 Paris Olympics, Biles guided the U.S. "Golden Girls" to team gold before capturing the individual all-around and vault titles, along with a silver on floor exercise. The four-medal haul in France lifted her Olympic tally to 11 medals, tying her with Věra Čáslavská as the second-most decorated female Olympic gymnast of all time, capped by an unprecedented journey from foster care to sporting immortality.
