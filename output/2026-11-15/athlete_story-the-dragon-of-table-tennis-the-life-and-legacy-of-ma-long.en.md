# The Dragon of Table Tennis: The Life and Legacy of Ma Long

*From a prodigious junior talent in Anshan to the most decorated Olympic table tennis player in history, explore the journey of the undisputed GOAT.*

**Athlete story** · 2026-11-15

## The Early Rise of a Prodigy

Born on 20 October 1988 in Anshan, Liaoning, Ma Long began a journey that would redefine the sport of table tennis. Named in the zodiac year of the dragon, he displayed an exceptional aptitude for the game from a young age. After securing both the Asian and World Junior Championships, he etched his name into the record books by becoming the youngest world champion at the age of 17, following his participation in the 2006 Bremen World Team Championship. His early development was guided by coaches Wang Hao and Ma Kai Xuan before he began training under Qin Zhijian.

Before turning 22, Ma demonstrated remarkable dominance on the ITTF World Tour, reaching the finals of 11 tournaments and winning eight of them. He also claimed two Asian Cup titles and two World Tour Grand Finals trophies. However, the path to greatness was not without severe roadblocks. Despite holding the world number one ranking for extended stretches between 2010 and 2014, he suffered a temporary dip in form following a 560-day win streak. A loss to Japan's Koki Niwa at the Asian Olympic Qualification Tournament and a defeat against Lee Sang-su at the 2012 Korea Open contributed to his omission from China's singles squad for the London Olympics.

## Overcoming Mental Hurdles and Breaking Through

National team coach Liu Guoliang publicly noted that while Ma possessed all the physical tools, he occasionally lacked the mental toughness required under intense pressure at major events. This scrutiny intensified after successive semifinal losses at the World Championships in 2009, 2011, and 2013, all against compatriot Wang Hao. While his rival Zhang Jike swiftly completed a career Grand Slam, Ma faced persistent questions regarding his ability to perform on the biggest stages.

Everything shifted during the 2013–2015 period. Following his third defeat to Wang Hao, Ma responded by capturing the China Open at two different venues, his third Asian Championships crown, and a grueling China National Games title against Fan Zhendong. In 2014, he dominated the World Team Table Tennis Championships in Tokyo without dropping a single game, earning the Victor Barna Award as the tournament's best player after defeating Timo Boll and Dimitrij Ovtcharov. The ultimate breakthrough arrived at the 2015 World Table Tennis Championships in Suzhou, where Ma triumphed over tournament sensation Fang Bo in six games to capture his first major singles world title outside of the World Cup.

## Immortalizing His Name in Rio and Beyond

By 2016, Ma reached the pinnacle of his sport. After securing the Qatar Open to break Wang Liqin's record for ITTF World Tour singles titles by a Chinese player, he secured his qualification for the 2016 Rio Olympics. In Brazil, after overcoming a scare against South Korea's Jeoung Young-sik and defeating Nigeria's Quadri Aruna and Japan's Jun Mizutani, Ma advanced to the singles final.

In a historic showdown against reigning Olympic champion Zhang Jike, Ma delivered a dominant 4–0 victory (14–12, 11–5, 11–4, 11–4) to claim the gold medal. The triumph made him the fifth male player to complete a career Grand Slam. Over his illustrious career, Ma held the world number one ranking for a total of 64 months—including a staggering 34 consecutive months starting in March 2015—establishing himself as a defining figure in global table tennis.
