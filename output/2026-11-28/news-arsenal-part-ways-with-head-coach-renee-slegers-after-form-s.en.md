# Arsenal Part Ways With Head Coach Renee Slegers After Form Slump

*The Dutch coach departs 18 months after winning the Champions League as the Gunners sit 12 points off the WSL summit.*

**News** · 2026-11-28

Arsenal parted company with head coach Renee Slegers by mutual consent on Friday, ending her two-year tenure despite delivering European and world honours to north London. Assistant coach Aaron D'Antino, who joined the club in August 2018, also left his post. Assistant Melissa Clarke-Reed has assumed interim charge while Arsenal conduct a search for a permanent successor.

The 37-year-old Dutch coach exits with Arsenal sixth in the Women's Super League after 11 fixtures. The Gunners sit 12 points behind league leaders Chelsea and eight points adrift of London City Lionesses in the third and final Women's Champions League qualification position. Arsenal's domestic slide includes a four-match winless sequence in the WSL and six consecutive games without a victory at Emirates Stadium.

## Goal drought seals managerial exit

A blunt attack proved central to Arsenal's downturn. The Gunners have registered 11 goals across 11 league matches this season, fewer than half the tallies managed by London City Lionesses (31), Chelsea (29), and second-placed Manchester City (28).

Mounting scrutiny preceded the exit following a 2-0 home defeat against Lyon in the Champions League and a league draw against West Ham. In the wake of the Lyon fixture, former Arsenal and England forward Lianne Sanderson publicly questioned the team's cohesion and direction, telling FourFourTwo that a leadership transition was required. Slegers herself conceded following Sunday's draw with West Ham that the team had failed to create enough attacking opportunities and accepted accountability for the club's poor run.

## From European silverware to domestic slide

The separation represents a rapid shift in fortunes. Slegers originally stepped in as interim manager in October 2024 before securing the permanent post three months later in January 2025. Her tenure produced silverware rapidly, securing Arsenal's second Women's Champions League crown in May 2025 and lifting the inaugural FIFA Women's Champions Cup in February 2026.

However, domestic standards could not be sustained. Arsenal director of women's football Clare Wheatley acknowledged that while Slegers provided historic milestones, recent on-pitch performance fell short of requirements, making a leadership reset unavoidable. Chief executive Richard Garlick echoed that sentiment, emphasizing the club's resolve to compete for silverware consistently across every campaign.
