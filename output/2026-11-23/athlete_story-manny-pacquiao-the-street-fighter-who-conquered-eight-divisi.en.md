# Manny Pacquiao: The Street Fighter Who Conquered Eight Divisions

*From sleeping on Manila pavements to winning belts across four decades, the Filipino icon built an unmatched legacy.*

**Athlete story** · 2026-11-23

Before Manny Pacquiao became the only eight-division champion in boxing history, he stood on construction sites in Manila at age 14, weighing less than 100 pounds and deciding whether his spare coins should purchase food or be mailed back to his mother in General Santos.

That struggle defined one of the most remarkable careers in world sport. Over a professional journey spanning three decades and 73 bouts, Pacquiao compiled a record of 62 wins, eight defeats and two draws, claiming 12 major world championships and becoming the only boxer to capture titles across four separate calendar decades.

## The Dirt Rings of General Santos

Emmanuel Dapidran Pacquiao was born on December 17, 1978, in Kibawe, Bukidnon, before his family relocated to General Santos in South Cotabato. He was the fourth of six children born to Rosalio Pacquiao and Dionisia Dapidran. When his parents separated during his sixth-grade school year, the household plunged into deep financial distress. Pacquiao completed his primary education at Saavedra Saway Elementary School, but crippling household poverty forced him to leave formal schooling behind as a teenager.

His path shifted in February 1990. At 11 years old, Pacquiao watched 42-to-1 underdog James "Buster" Douglas knock out undisputed heavyweight champion Mike Tyson in Tokyo. His maternal uncle, Sardo Mejia, noticed the boy's fascination and constructed a makeshift gym in a home backyard. Six months later, Pacquiao was competing in public park fights in General Santos, earning small cash prizes that bought sacks of rice.

By 15, he had established himself as the top amateur junior in Mindanao, compiling a 60–4 amateur record. Seeking greater competition and income to support his family, he boarded a boat to Manila as a stowaway, working day jobs on building sites and sleeping in boxing gyms before making his paid debut in January 1995 as a 106-pound junior flyweight at age 16.

## Conquering Eight Divisions

Pacquiao claimed his first world championship on December 4, 1998, knocking out Chatchai Sasakul in Thailand to capture the WBC flyweight title just two weeks shy of his 20th birthday. Yet it was his move to the United States in 2001, pairing with trainer Freddie Roach at the Wild Card Gym in Hollywood, that triggered his meteoric rise.

Stepping in on two weeks' notice in June 2001, Pacquiao stopped IBF super bantamweight champion Lehlohonolo Ledwaba in Las Vegas. Over the next decade, his hand speed and southpaw angles dismantled a generation of future Hall of Famers. He stopped Marco Antonio Barrera in 2003, engaged in a celebrated trilogy with Érik Morales, and contested four dramatic bouts against Mexican rival Juan Manuel Márquez.

As Pacquiao added muscle without sacrificing punching speed, he shattered conventional limits on weight climbing. In December 2008, he moved up two divisions to dismantle Oscar De La Hoya, forcing the boxing superstar to retire on his stool after eight rounds. Five months later, in May 2009, he knocked Ricky Hatton unconscious in round two at the MGM Grand to capture the Ring light welterweight crown.

By the time he punished Antonio Margarito over 12 brutal rounds for the WBC super welterweight belt in November 2010, Pacquiao had secured world titles across eight distinct weight classes—a feat unmatched in boxing annals. He captured lineal championships in four weight categories (flyweight, featherweight, super featherweight and light welterweight), and collected belts in four of the sport's original divisions.

Longevity remained his signature trademark. In July 2019, at 40 years and seven months old, Pacquiao handed Keith Thurman his first professional defeat in Las Vegas, capturing the WBA welterweight crown to become the oldest welterweight titleholder in boxing history.

## Life Beyond the Ropes

Outside the ring, Pacquiao transformed his fame into business ventures and civic leadership. He earned his secondary school equivalency diploma in February 2007 before entering public office. Voters in Sarangani elected him to the Philippine House of Representatives in 2010, where he served two terms before winning a nationwide seat in the Philippine Senate in 2016.

Between legislative sessions and world title camps, he played and coached in the Philippine Basketball Association for Kia from 2014 to 2017, later founding the semi-professional Maharlika Pilipinas Basketball League. In music, his recording of "Sometimes When We Touch" climbed to number 19 on the US Billboard Adult Contemporary chart.

Pacquiao retired from boxing in September 2021 to mount a campaign for the Philippine presidency, finishing third in the May 2022 election won by Ferdinand Marcos Jr. After stepping back from electoral contests following the 2025 midterm cycle, Marcos named him head of the National Anti-Poverty Commission in 2026—bringing the former street laborer full circle to address the systemic deprivation that first sent him into a boxing ring.
