The United States and the West Indies are being lined up to co-host the 2034 men's T20 World Cup, The Guardian reported, ten years after the two shared the 2024 tournament. The decision is not final: the International Cricket Council still lists the 2034 hosts as "to be confirmed".

At its board meetings in Bali from 11 to 15 November 2026, the ICC approved the windows for its events up to 2035. The 2034 men's T20 World Cup is set for 6 July to 6 August, a northern-summer slot that, according to The Guardian, rules out venues in Asia, Africa and Oceania. England, Ireland and Scotland will host the 2030 edition in the same part of the year, and the England and Wales Cricket Board is understood not to be bidding for 2034, the newspaper reported. That leaves a joint North American and Caribbean event as the preferred option.

## Bigger plans for the US

The United States is seen as especially important to the ICC, partly because cricket will be played at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, The Guardian reported. At the 2024 World Cup, the US staged 16 matches at three venues, in Grand Prairie (Texas), Lauderhill (Florida) and a temporary stadium in Nassau County, New York, while the West Indies hosted the other 39.

For 2034, the venues being discussed include the temporary Olympic cricket ground in Pomona, California, Frederick near Washington DC, the Oakland Coliseum and Marymoor Park in Seattle, as well as the 2024 grounds in Texas and Florida, according to The Guardian.

The two boards are already working together. West Indies are set to host India for two T20 internationals in North America in July 2027, with Washington DC and Toronto among the front-runners to stage them, ESPN reported.

## USA Cricket's problems first

Any award depends on USA Cricket sorting out its governance. The ICC has suspended the national body, ESPNcricinfo reported, and USA Cricket filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in 2025. According to The Guardian, the ICC has lent it money to keep staff working, and the owners of Major League Cricket have put up $3 million (£2.3 million) for a rescue plan, which is expected to be settled next year.

The ICC plans to finalise how it awards future tournaments in 2027, and hosting rights for events after 2031 are likely to be handed out from 2028.
