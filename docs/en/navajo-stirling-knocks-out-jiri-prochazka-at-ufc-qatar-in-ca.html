Navajo Stirling Knocks Out Jiri Prochazka at UFC Qatar in Career-Defining Win

The unbeaten New Zealand light-heavyweight stopped the former champion with two seconds left in round two to move to 12-0.

What happened

Navajo Stirling delivered a statement victory on Saturday, November 21, 2026, knocking out former light-heavyweight champion Jiří Procházka in the main event of UFC Fight Night in Qatar. The 29-year-old New Zealander controlled distance with leg kicks across the first two rounds before landing a decisive right hook with two seconds left in the second frame. Procházka hit the canvas at the 4:58 mark, and referee Marc Goddard stopped the contest following a brief flurry of ground strikes. The defeat marked the Czech veteran's second straight loss, while Procházka acknowledged Stirling was "the better fighter" on the night.

Why it is being talked about

Stirling's finish capped an explosive calendar year that places him in contention for the UFC's fighter of the year honours. After dispatching former divisional king Jan Błachowicz in August, Stirling took out another former world champion in Procházka without absorbing significant damage. The clinical performance pushed the Auckland-based striker into the elite tier of the 205-pound division after just four years as a professional mixed martial artist. However, his swift ascent introduces a major logistical puzzle for the UFC: reigning light-heavyweight champion Carlos Ulberg is Stirling's long-time teammate and close friend at New Zealand's City Kickboxing gym.

Key facts and numbers

- 12-0: Stirling's unblemished professional mixed martial arts record.
- 4: Consecutive knockout victories for Stirling in 2026.
- 4:58: The official time in round two when referee Marc Goddard waved off the fight.
- $100,000: Performance bonus awarded to Stirling following the headline stoppage in Qatar.

What happens next

Stirling's immediate path to the title remains complicated by gym loyalty. Ulberg, who dethroned Procházka in April 2026, is currently rehabilitating a torn anterior cruciate ligament suffered in that bout and has not competed since. Teammates rarely agree to fight each other, and Stirling distanced himself from calling out Ulberg inside the Octagon, stating: "I'm not too sure yet, we'll figure something out and see who's above me." UFC matchmakers must now decide whether to match Stirling against another top contender or position him for an interim title clash while Ulberg works toward full fitness.
