Chan Vathanaka: The Kampot Prodigy Who Put Cambodian Football on the Regional Map

From Kampot to Japan and the Cambodian Premier League scoring record, then two ACL tears: the story of Cambodia's most prolific forward and the comeback he is still writing.

M
Mak · Athlete stories
25 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · AI-assisted · reviewed by the author
ShareTelegramFacebookXWhatsApp

Chan Vathanaka was born on 23 January 1994 in southern Cambodia's coastal province of Kampot. Armed with an incisive left foot, deceptive acceleration and dead-ball precision, he grew into the defining attacker of modern Cambodian football. Widely known across Southeast Asia under the moniker "CV11", Vathanaka transformed from a teenage prospect in Svay Rieng into an international standard-bearer who broke national scoring records and ventured into professional leagues in Japan and Malaysia.

His structured football education began in 2010 when he joined the youth academy of Preah Khan Reach Svay Rieng at age 16. He made his senior league debut during the 2011–12 Cambodian League season, scoring 10 goals in 17 appearances. That breakthrough prompted domestic giants Boeung Ket to secure his signature in June 2012. Vathanaka immediately validated the move by winning the Cambodian League Best Young Player of the Year award in 2012, followed by the Hun Sen Cup Golden Boot in 2013 and his first Cambodian League Player of the Season award in 2014.

The Record-Shattering Boeung Ket Era

The 2015 campaign established Vathanaka as a regional phenomenon. On 11 July 2015, he struck eight goals in a 12–2 rout of Kirivong Sok Sen Chey, establishing the highest single-match tally by a Cambodian-born player in league history and reaching 16 goals in his first eight matches of the season. Three weeks later, on 1 August 2015, he scored all six goals in a 6–0 win against Asia Europe United. In regional competition at the 2015 Mekong Club Championship, Vathanaka netted a hat-trick to defeat Vietnamese champions Becamex Binh Duong 3–2 in the semi-finals, before Boeung Ket finished as runners-up to Thailand's Buriram United. He concluded the tournament as top scorer with five goals and collected the Most Valuable Player award.

Vathanaka closed 2015 with 35 domestic league goals and 55 goals across all club and national-team competitions, the highest total recorded by any Southeast Asian footballer that calendar year.

His scoring rate remained relentless in 2016. In his first start of that league season, Vathanaka struck a hat-trick in under five minutes in a 6–0 win over CMAC FC. On 21 May 2016 against Svay Rieng, Boeung Ket trailed 1–0 before Vathanaka scored in the 85th, 89th and 93rd minutes—the first a left-foot volley from a high cross—to seal a 3–1 win. He topped the league scoring charts for a second straight year with 22 goals, guiding Boeung Ket to the 2016 Cambodian League championship by a single point on the final matchday and capturing both the league Golden Boot and Player of the Season honors.

Pioneering Stints in Japan and Malaysia

Vathanaka's domestic dominance opened rare foreign doors for Cambodian athletes. In January 2017, he signed on loan with J3 League outfit Fujieda MYFC in Japan, taking his customary number 11 shirt. The transition proved demanding; physical and tactical adaptations limited his game time, and he departed without starting a match, making a single late substitute appearance in the J3 League.

He moved south in December 2017, signing with Malaysia Super League side Pahang FA for the 2018 campaign. He made 10 league appearances and scored once before the club released him midway through the season to register Canadian forward Issey Nakajima. Following a brief return to Boeung Ket, where he contributed to another domestic title run, Vathanaka returned to Malaysia on 18 January 2019 by signing a free contract with PKNS FC.

At PKNS, he switched to jersey number 29. His second Malaysian stint proved more productive: he made over 13 league appearances, including a goal against his former club Pahang in April 2019.

International Milestones and Overcoming Adversity

On the international stage, Vathanaka debuted for Cambodia's senior national team on 22 March 2013 in an AFC Challenge Cup qualifier against Turkmenistan. During the 2018 FIFA World Cup qualifiers on 12 March 2015, he entered off the bench against Macau at the Phnom Penh Olympic Stadium to score twice, including a 30-yard free kick, securing a 3–0 first-leg win. In July 2016, he curled home another direct free kick in a friendly against Singapore. At the 2016 AFF Championship, Vathanaka scored both Cambodian goals in a 3–2 group-stage loss to Malaysia, later adding two more goals against Laos at the 2020 AFF Championship.

Off the pitch, Vathanaka established rituals deeply tied to family and culture. After an injury layoff in September 2015, he spent seven hours recording songs in a studio with Cambodian singer Meas Soksophea. In October 2015, he began celebrating his goals by kissing a tattoo on his right arm, inked as a tribute to his parents and family. His profile led to commercial recognition, including an appointment as goodwill ambassador for telecommunications firm SEATEL in January 2016.

Two Knees, Two Comebacks

Injuries have shaped the second half of his career. On 26 January 2022, in a pre-season cup match against Visakha, Vathanaka tore the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) in his right knee. A surgical team from Bangkok Hospital's BASEM sports medicine centre flew to Cambodia to reconstruct it, and he did not play competitive football again until 5 March 2023, more than a year later.

He answered with eight league goals in the 2023–24 Cambodian Premier League season as Boeung Ket finished fourth. Then the same blow struck the other leg. On 22 January 2025, in a Hun Sen Cup round-of-16 tie that Boeung Ket won 8–1 against Kompong Cham, he tore the ACL and meniscus in his left knee. He had surgery the next day.

A second year of rehabilitation followed. In January 2026, days before his 32nd birthday, Boeung Ket named him in their squad again, and he was back on the scoresheet from the penalty spot during the club's Hun Sen Cup run, which ended with a share of third place. His league minutes have been carefully managed since: official Cambodian Premier League records show one appearance, as a substitute, in the 2025–26 season.

The Numbers That Still Stand

No knee injury can erase what came before. Vathanaka is the all-time top scorer of the Cambodian Premier League with 143 goals. He was its Player of the Season three years in a row (2014, 2015 and 2016) and its top scorer in 2015 and 2016. His best years came between the ages of 20 and 25, when his dribbling down the left and his left foot from dead balls made him one of the most dangerous wide forwards in Southeast Asia.

A Final Chapter Still Being Written

At 32, still wearing Boeung Ket's number 11, Vathanaka is no longer the explosive young forward who tore through defences in 2015. The question for Cambodian football is not whether he can repeat a 37-goal season, but how much of that spark two rebuilt knees still allow, and whether a fit, sharp CV11 can still decide big matches from the left flank.

For a generation of Cambodian fans who grew up watching him run at defenders, he remains the player who showed what Cambodian football could look like at its boldest. Every minute he plays now is a chance to prove that the story of the Kampot prodigy has one more chapter in it.
