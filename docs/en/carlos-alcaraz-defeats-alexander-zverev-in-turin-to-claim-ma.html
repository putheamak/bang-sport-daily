Carlos Alcaraz Defeats Alexander Zverev in Turin to Claim Maiden ATP Finals Title

The 23-year-old Spaniard joins Agassi, Federer and Djokovic as the fourth man to win all four Grand Slams and the year-end championship.

ShareTelegramFacebookXWhatsApp

What happened

On Sunday, 22 November 2026, Carlos Alcaraz defeated world No. 1 Alexander Zverev 6-2, 7-6 (9-7) in the final of the Nitto ATP Finals at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. The 23-year-old Spaniard broke Zverev's serve four times during a match that lasted two hours and one minute. In the second-set tiebreak, Zverev erased an early deficit to create three set points, but Alcaraz erased all three before converting his second championship point with a backhand winner to claim the season-ending trophy for the first time in his career.

Why it is being talked about

The victory makes Alcaraz only the fourth male player in tennis history to win all four Grand Slam tournaments and the ATP Finals, joining Andre Agassi, Roger Federer and Novak Djokovic. At 23 years of age, he is the youngest man to complete that combination of titles. The outcome in Turin also delivered an emphatic conclusion to the 2026 season: while Zverev won Roland Garros and the US Open to finish the year ranked world No. 1, Alcaraz defeated him head-to-head in the season finale. "You are an absolute legend of our sport, and you are only 23," Zverev told Alcaraz during the trophy ceremony.

Key facts and numbers

Alcaraz went through the tournament with an undefeated 5-0 match record, dropping only a single set across wins over Frances Tiafoe, Daniil Medvedev, Flavio Cobolli, Ben Shelton and Zverev. By finishing unbeaten, the Spaniard collected a record payout of $5.171 million from the event's $15.75 million prize pool, along with 1,500 ATP ranking points.

The trophy was Alcaraz's 30th career tour-level singles title and his sixth of 2026, adding to autumn tournament wins in Tokyo, Vienna and the Paris Masters. Alcaraz concluded his 2026 campaign with a 47-5 win-loss record despite missing four and a half months during the spring and summer because of a right wrist injury. Zverev finished his year with a 69-win record, 10,320 points and the year-end No. 1 ranking.

What happens next

The ATP Tour now enters its brief winter off-season before the 2027 calendar begins in Australia in January. Alcaraz finishes 2026 ranked world No. 3, trailing world No. 2 Jannik Sinner by 350 points and Zverev at the top of the standings. Sinner, who missed the Turin finale with a knee injury scheduled for surgery on 24 November, is expected to return alongside Alcaraz and Zverev for the Australian Open.
