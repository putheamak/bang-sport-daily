Pitchamon Opatniputh Banned Three Months by BWF After Positive Doping Test

The 2023 world junior champion tested positive for furosemide ahead of the Asian Games in Nagoya, with her suspension backdated to expire on 20 December

ShareTelegramFacebookXWhatsApp

The Badminton World Federation (BWF) has handed Thailand's 19-year-old women's singles player Pitchamon Opatniputh a three-month suspension following an anti-doping rule violation, the governing body confirmed on Friday, 27 November 2026. Pitchamon tested positive for furosemide during an out-of-competition test conducted by the International Testing Agency (ITA) on 17 September 2026 in Nagoya, Japan, two days before the opening ceremony of the Asian Games.

The finding in Nagoya and the BWF ruling

Furosemide is prohibited at all times under section S5 of the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) prohibited list as a diuretic and masking agent, substances that can facilitate rapid weight loss through fluid excretion or conceal the presence of other banned substances. Following the adverse analytical finding in her A-sample, Pitchamon requested the analysis of her B-sample, which was opened on 24 September 2026 in the presence of an independent witness and confirmed the presence of the substance. She accepted the violation under Olympic Council of Asia (OCA) rules, leading to her exclusion from the Asian Games and the withdrawal of her accreditation.

The BWF confirmed that her three-month sanction was backdated to begin from the date of her provisional suspension in September. "The matter has now concluded, with a three-month period of ineligibility commencing from her provisional suspension. Her period of ineligibility will expire on 20 December 2026," the BWF stated on its website. Thai sports minister Surasak Phancharoenworakul stated after the initial test that Pitchamon believed the substance originated from an anti-inflammatory ointment she had applied.

Ranking slide and immediate pathway back to the court

Pitchamon, who won the BWF World Junior Championships women's singles title in 2023, sat at world No. 18 when the provisional ban was imposed, though her absence from autumn tournaments has dropped her to No. 22. Prior to the sanction, she reached the final of the Indonesia Masters Super 500, losing to China's Chen Yufei, and finished as runner-up to Japan's Nozomi Okuhara at the Orleans Masters Super 300. Her senior titles on tour include the 2023 Malaysia Masters Super 100 and the 2025 Baoji China Masters Super 100.

Because the three-month period concludes on 20 December 2026, Pitchamon is cleared to return to competition on 21 December, leaving her eligible for the start of the 2027 BWF World Tour calendar in January. Neither Pitchamon nor the Badminton Association of Thailand issued an additional public response following Friday's BWF confirmation.
