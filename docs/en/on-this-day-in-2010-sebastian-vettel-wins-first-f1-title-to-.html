On This Day in 2010: Sebastian Vettel wins first F1 title to become youngest champion

Red Bull Racing's German star secured the Drivers' Championship at the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, launching a four-year era of dominance for the team.

ShareTelegramFacebookXWhatsApp

On 14 November 2010, Sebastian Vettel cemented his status as one of motorsport's rising prodigies by winning the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix at the Yas Marina Circuit. The victory secured his first World Drivers' Championship and crowned him the youngest champion in Formula One history at the age of 23. Driving for Red Bull Racing, the German driver entered the final round of the 2010 season facing a tight championship battle against rival contenders. By converting his pole position into a commanding race victory, Vettel bypassed championship leader Fernando Alonso in the standings, capturing the title that launched an era of dominance for the Austrian-licensed, English-based team.

The Rise of a Prodigy

Vettel's journey to the 2010 crown began within the Red Bull Junior Team, which he accepted an invitation to join in 1998 during his karting years. After dominating junior categories, including 18 victories from 20 races in the 2004 Formula BMW ADAC championship, he made his Formula One weekend debut as a test driver with BMW Sauber in 2006 at the Turkish Grand Prix, where he set the fastest time in the second Friday free practice at 19 years and 53 days old. He later transitioned to Red Bull's sister team, Toro Rosso, where he secured his maiden victory at the 2008 Italian Grand Prix to become the then-youngest driver to win a Formula One Grand Prix.

Promoted to the senior Red Bull Racing squad in 2009 alongside Adrian Newey's technical department, Vettel finished runner-up to Jenson Button before breaking through for his maiden world title a year later. The triumph marked the first of four consecutive Drivers' and Constructors' Championship titles for Red Bull between 2010 and 2013, achieved during their partnership with Renault engines and establishing the first Austrian team to win the title. Red Bull had previously purchased Jaguar Racing on 15 November 2004 for a symbolic US$1 with a commitment to invest US$400 million over three seasons, installing Christian Horner as team boss.

A Lasting Legacy in the Sport

When Vettel retired from Formula One at the end of the 2022 season following stints with BMW Sauber, Toro Rosso, Red Bull, Ferrari, and Aston Martin, he left behind statistics that placed him among the sport's all-time greats. Across 16 seasons, he accumulated 53 Grand Prix victories, 57 pole positions, 38 fastest laps, and 122 podium finishes. He also won the Race of Champions in 2015 and the Nations' Cup six consecutive times from 2007 to 2012 representing Germany alongside Michael Schumacher.

The historic night at the Yas Marina Circuit remains a defining milestone for Red Bull Racing as well, establishing the team as a powerhouse before subsequent championship eras led by Max Verstappen. Vettel's record-breaking achievement in 2010 continues to stand as a benchmark for young drivers breaking into the pinnacle of motorsport.
