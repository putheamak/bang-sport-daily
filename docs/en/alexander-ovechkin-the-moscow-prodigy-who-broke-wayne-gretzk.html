Alexander Ovechkin: The Moscow Prodigy Who Broke Wayne Gretzky's NHL Record

From a childhood loss in Moscow to the NHL goals record and a farewell season: the story of the Washington Capitals captain.

Mak · Athlete stories
28 Nov 2026, 14:27 (GMT+7) · 3 min read · AI-assisted · reviewed by the author
ShareTelegramFacebookXWhatsApp

On 6 April 2025, at UBS Arena in Elmont, New York, Alexander Ovechkin scored a second-period power-play goal past New York Islanders goaltender Ilya Sorokin. It was the 895th regular-season goal of his NHL career and broke Wayne Gretzky's record of 894, which had stood since Gretzky retired in 1999. After the game, Gretzky shook his hand during an on-ice ceremony for the 39-year-old Washington Capitals captain. Sorokin was the 183rd different goaltender Ovechkin had scored against, ESPN reported.

A sporting family in Moscow

Ovechkin was born on 17 September 1985 in Moscow. His mother, Tatyana Ovechkina, won Olympic gold with the Soviet basketball team in 1976 and 1980, and his father, Mikhail, played football for Dynamo Moscow. He picked up a hockey stick at the age of two and later joined the Dynamo Moscow youth academy.

His older brother Sergei often took him to training. When Ovechkin was 10, Sergei, then in his early twenties, died of a blood clot after a car accident, Sports Illustrated reported.

As a boy in the Dynamo system, Ovechkin scored 56 goals in one season, beating the club academy record of 53 held by Pavel Bure, according to Wikipedia.

From Dynamo to Washington

Ovechkin made his senior debut for Dynamo Moscow in the Russian Superleague in 2001–02, aged 16. In four seasons he scored 36 goals and added 32 assists in 152 games, and helped Dynamo win the 2004–05 Russian title. A year earlier, in 2004, the Washington Capitals had made him the first overall pick in the NHL Draft.

After the 2004–05 NHL season was cancelled by a lockout, Ovechkin made his NHL debut on 5 October 2005 and scored twice against the Columbus Blue Jackets in a 3–2 win. On 16 January 2006 against the Phoenix Coyotes, he was knocked to the ice by Paul Mara and, while sliding on his back, hooked the puck past goaltender Brian Boucher with one hand, a goal commentators still call "The Goal". He finished his rookie season with 52 goals and 54 assists and won the Calder Trophy as the league's best rookie.

Trophies and the Stanley Cup

Washington named Ovechkin captain on 5 January 2010. He won the Hart Trophy as league MVP in 2008, 2009 and 2013, the players' award for the most outstanding player three years in a row from 2008 to 2010 (the Lester B. Pearson Award, renamed the Ted Lindsay Award in 2010), and the Maurice "Rocket" Richard Trophy as the top goal scorer nine times. With Russia he won the World Championship in 2008, 2012 and 2014.

His long wait for a title ended on 7 June 2018 at T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas, when the Capitals beat the Vegas Golden Knights 4–3 in Game 5 to win the franchise's first Stanley Cup. Ovechkin scored 15 goals in the playoffs and won the Conn Smythe Trophy as playoff MVP.

The record and the last season

In 2024–25 he missed 16 games with a fractured left fibula and still scored 44 goals in 65 games, including the record-breaking 895th, which also made him the NHL's all-time leader in power-play goals. On 22 March 2026 against Colorado he scored his 1,000th NHL goal including the playoffs, NHL.com reported, a milestone previously reached only by Gretzky.

On 5 October 2026, 21 years to the day after his NHL debut, Ovechkin announced that the 2026–27 season would be his last in the NHL. He began it with 929 regular-season goals, according to NHL.com, and remains Washington's captain in his farewell season.
