Marc Marquez Announces MotoGP Retirement Days After Winning Record-Equalling Eighth Title

The 33-year-old Spaniard will race for the last time at this weekend's Valencia Grand Prix, level with Giacomo Agostini on eight premier-class titles.

ShareTelegramFacebookXWhatsApp

Eight-time MotoGP world champion Marc Marquez announced his retirement on Friday, 27 November 2026, at the Circuit Ricardo Tormo in Valencia, Spain. This weekend's Valencia Grand Prix, the last round of the season, will be the final race of his career.

Cards in the Ducati garage

Marquez broke the news in the Ducati Lenovo Team garage at the start of the first practice session, holding up handwritten cards to the live television cameras. The first card read "We'll talk later but first", Crash.net reported. The cards went on to say "This will be my last race in MotoGP" and "We made history", according to AP.

"It is official. Marc Marquez has decided to retire from MotoGP," Ducati said. MotoGP called him a rider who "bows out on top as reigning Champion".

At a press conference at the circuit later on Friday, the 33-year-old explained his choice. "I arrived in MotoGP in my way, and I will go in my way," he said, as quoted by Crash.net. He added that "the situation this year was very in the limit". According to Crash.net, he made the decision after the Japanese Grand Prix at Motegi in October, and only his brother Alex knew.

A title won from 102 points behind

Marquez clinched the 2026 championship five days earlier at the Portuguese Grand Prix in Portimão on 22 November, where eighth place was enough. The title drew him level with Giacomo Agostini's record of eight premier-class crowns and moved him one ahead of Valentino Rossi. Counting his 125cc title in 2010 and his Moto2 title in 2012, it was his tenth world championship, Anadolu Agency reported.

The season began badly. Marquez fell 102 points behind Aprilia's Marco Bezzecchi, a deficit that AP and MCNews describe as the largest ever overturned in MotoGP. He has won seven Grands Prix and ten Sprint races this season and took the title with a round to spare.

Injuries behind the decision

The announcement surprised the paddock because Marquez signed a contract extension in June 2026 covering 2027 and 2028, the first seasons of the new 850cc rules. His career was shaped by injuries: he broke his right humerus at Jerez in 2020 and needed four operations, according to MotoGP, and he also needed surgery on his right shoulder after Bezzecchi collided with him on the first lap of the Indonesian Grand Prix, Motorsport.com reported.

MotoGP said the Valencia weekend will celebrate his career under the hashtag #ThankYouMarc.
