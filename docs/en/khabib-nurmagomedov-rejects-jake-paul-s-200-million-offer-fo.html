Khabib Nurmagomedov Rejects Jake Paul's $200 Million Offer for MMA Return

The undefeated former UFC lightweight champion insists money cannot motivate a comeback, reaffirming his retirement.

M
Mak · Combat sports
20 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · AI-assisted · reviewed by the author
ShareTelegramFacebookXWhatsApp

Former UFC lightweight champion Khabib Nurmagomedov has flatly turned down a $200 million offer from boxer and promoter Jake Paul to return to professional mixed martial arts. In video footage released on Paul's digital channels on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, the undefeated Russian fighter dismissed the proposal on the spot.

"When are you coming back? $200 million?" Paul asked during a backstage conversation recorded after a Professional Fighters League event.

"Why I have to come back?" Nurmagomedov responded. "I have a lot of people, they are best in the world. Nothing to prove. There is no opponent, only money. It's not motivation for me."

Paul countered by arguing that competing under his promotional banner would "change MMA forever" by establishing better financial conditions for athletes, but Nurmagomedov did not budge. The lighthearted exchange concluded with Paul playfully attempting a takedown against the wrestling specialist.

A Legacy Sealed at 29-0

Nurmagomedov retired from active competition in October 2020 at UFC 254 in Abu Dhabi after submitting Justin Gaethje with a second-round triangle choke. The victory moved his career record to 29 wins and zero losses. He won the vacant UFC lightweight belt against Al Iaquinta in April 2018, before headlining UFC 229 against Conor McGregor in October 2018, an event that drew 2.4 million pay-per-view buys to remain the most lucrative card in MMA history.

Following the death of his father and coach Abdulmanap Nurmagomedov in July 2020, Khabib promised his mother he would never fight again without him in his corner. He was formally inducted into the Modern Wing of the UFC Hall of Fame in July 2022.

Focus on Coaching the Next Generation

Nurmagomedov's steadfast refusal matches his responses to UFC chief executive Dana White, who confirmed on November 14 that every effort to orchestrate a comeback had failed.

Since stepping away from competition, Nurmagomedov has dedicated his time to coaching at American Kickboxing Academy, cornering two-division champion Islam Makhachev and bantamweight contender Umar Nurmagomedov. While Paul continues to recruit marquee talent following the integration of Most Valuable Promotions and the PFL, Nurmagomedov reaffirmed that money alone will not reopen the door to the cage.
