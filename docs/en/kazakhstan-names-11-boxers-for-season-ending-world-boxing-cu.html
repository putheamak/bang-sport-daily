Kazakhstan Names 11 Boxers for Season-Ending World Boxing Cup Finals in Tashkent

Karina Ibragimova leads a squad of six men and five women heading to Uzbekistan for the 31-nation tournament.

M
Mak · Combat sports
24 Nov 2026 · 1 min read · AI-assisted · reviewed by the author
ShareTelegramFacebookXWhatsApp

Kazakhstan's Boxing Federation and National Olympic Committee announced on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, an 11-boxer roster for the season-ending World Boxing Cup Finals in Tashkent, Uzbekistan.

The six-day competition runs from 28 November to 3 December at the Martial Arts Arena in Tashkent's Olympic Village. A total of 142 boxers from 31 nations will contest 20 weight categories—ten for men and ten for women—with brackets capped at eight competitors per division following qualification stages earlier this year in Brazil and China.

Youth champions and veteran leadership

Three-time World Championship medallist and Paris 2024 Olympian Karina Ibragimova headlines the Kazakh women's selection at 57kg. She is joined in the women's draw by Bakhyt Seidish (70kg), Shugyla Nalibay (75kg), Valeriya Aksenova (80kg), and Dina Islambekova (+80kg). Both Aksenova and Islambekova secured their berths after claiming bronze medals at the Guiyang stage in June.

The men's lineup includes Daniyal Sabit (50kg), Farukh Toktasynov (70kg), Aman Konysbekov (75kg), Sultanbek Aibaruly (85kg), and Daulet Tolemisov (90kg). In the 80kg category, 2024 world youth champion Oner Seilkhan was drafted into the squad after defeating fellow squad hopeful Dias Molzhigitov by a 3-2 split decision at the national championships in Taraz earlier this month.

Central Asian rivalry moves to Tashkent

The Tashkent tournament renews the long-standing ring rivalry between Kazakhstan and host nation Uzbekistan, who entered an 18-boxer delegation that features men's 80kg world champion Javokhir Ummataliev and heavyweight Jahongir Zokirov.

Kazakhstan enjoyed strong momentum across the 2026 World Boxing Cup circuit. At the opening leg in Foz do Iguaçu, Brazil, in April, Kazakh fighters claimed three gold, one silver, and three bronze medals. Two months later in Guiyang, China, the team took five golds and three bronzes, finishing second in the medal table behind hosts China while eclipsing Uzbekistan's four gold medals.

The official tournament draw takes place on 27 November. Quarter-final bouts begin the following afternoon, leading to all 20 gold-medal finals on 3 December, with tournament ranking points directly affecting seeding for the 2027 and 2028 Olympic qualification cycle.
